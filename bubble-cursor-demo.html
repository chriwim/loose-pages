Bubble Cursor

A cursor whose circle grows and shrinks so that it always captures exactly one target: the closest one. You stop aiming at a small target and aim at the space around it.

Introduced by Tovi Grossman and Ravin Balakrishnan. Move the pointer over the demo below.

Try it

Click the orange target. Time and misses are tracked for each cursor, so switch between them and compare. On a touch screen, drag to move the bubble and lift to select.

How the radius is chosen

The bubble recomputes its size every time the pointer moves. It works from two distances measured to every target on screen.

ConDist is the distance from the pointer to a target's nearest edge. IntDist is the distance to its farthest edge. A bubble with radius ConDist just touches the target. A bubble with radius IntDist fully contains it.

R = min( IntDist1, ConDist2 )

  1. Find the nearest target

    Rank targets by ConDist. Target 1 has the smallest value. It is the one you will select, wherever the pointer is.

  2. Set two limits

    The bubble should be big enough to cover target 1 completely, which is IntDist1. It must stay small enough not to touch target 2, the second nearest, which is ConDist2.

  3. Take the smaller limit

    The radius is the smaller of the two, so at most one target ever intersects the bubble. Turn on Show distances to see both rings and which one wins.

  4. Stretch to cover the target

    When ConDist2 is the smaller limit, the bubble stops short of covering target 1. A secondary shape then reaches out and wraps the target, so you can see what a click will pick.

Why it helps

Because the nearest edge always wins, every pixel belongs to exactly one target. A target's selectable region is no longer its own outline but its cell of space. Turn on Show selection zones to see the cells.

Targets with empty space around them get large cells and become easy to hit. Targets packed together get small cells, but each one still owns the space up to its neighbours. In the paper's studies the bubble cursor significantly outperformed the point cursor, and its performance could be predicted with Fitts' law using an effective target width that grows with the free space around a target.

Things to try

  • Pick Fixed circle and move into a crowded area. Several targets sit under the circle at once, so the pick is ambiguous. The bubble avoids this by shrinking.
  • Pick Point and raise the target count. Small targets get hard to hit. Switch back to Bubble and the same layout feels easy.
  • Park the pointer in an empty gap with distances on. Watch which ring is smaller as you drift toward one target.

This demo uses circular targets. The stretching shape is a simplified drawing of the paper's secondary bubble.
